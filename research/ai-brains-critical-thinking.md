# AI, brains, and critical thinking

Research date: 2026-11-11. Built from very recent Hermes vault clippings (created 2026-11-11) plus the MIT Media Lab preprint that HealthyGamerGG quotes. Labels: *verified fact* | *nuance / caveat* | *claim* | *technical suggestion*.

Curated course links: [[../course/syllabus/links-from-clippings]]. Bibliography: [[../course/syllabus/references]].

## Verdict (teach this)

1. **Well supported:** generative AI shifts work from *doing* toward *verifying and supervising*. Higher trust in the tool correlates with less self-reported critical scrutiny (Lee et al., CHI 2025).
2. **Reasonably supported:** *order of use* matters. Forming a view first, then consulting the model, engages people differently (and better on recall/ownership signals) than model-first (Kosmyna et al. crossover; Gerlich structured-prompt work).
3. **Suggestive, over-read in media:** the MIT EEG study shows weaker connectivity and lower ownership for LLM-assisted essay writers in a small sample. "Cognitive debt" and "brain rust" are framings, not proven lasting damage.
4. **Not established:** that everyday AI use causes durable, general decline in critical-thinking ability. Cross-sectional correlations (e.g. Gerlich survey) cannot show direction.
5. **Separate risk track:** sycophantic chatbots can reinforce delusions and corrode interpersonal repair (case reports, Cheng et al., Haber overview). That is mental-health harm, not the same claim as "AI makes everyone stupider."

For Critical Vibe Coding: treat judgment, critique, and verification as the skills the course practices on purpose. Offload volume; keep the decision.

## Primary study HealthyGamerGG leans on

*verified fact:* Kosmyna, N., et al. (2025). *Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task.* arXiv:2506.08872. MIT Media Lab project page: https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/your-brain-on-chatgpt/

| Design piece | What they did |
|---|---|
| Groups | LLM, search engine, brain-only (no tools) |
| Sessions 1 to 3 | Same condition each time; N = 54 |
| Session 4 crossover | N = 18; LLM users switched to brain-only and vice versa |
| Measures | EEG connectivity during essay writing; NLP on essays; human + AI scoring; ownership and quote recall |

Compressed findings (as stated by authors / Media Lab abstract):

- Brain-only: strongest, most distributed neural networks.
- Search: moderate.
- LLM: weakest connectivity; lowest essay ownership; weak ability to quote own text.
- Crossover: Brain-to-LLM showed higher memory recall and stronger prefrontal / occipito-parietal activation than LLM-to-Brain.

*nuance / caveat:* Authors and Media Lab mark the work as **not peer-reviewed** at first posting. Sample for the crossover is small. EEG differences during a task ≠ proven long-term cognitive decline. Independent commentary (e.g. arXiv:2601.00856) flags power, multiple comparisons, and over-interpretation risk.

*claim (HealthyGamerGG):* video *How AI is Killing Your Potential* (2025-12-20) calls the pattern "brain rust" (use it or lose it) and warns that AI-written student work improves *output* while shrinking practice that builds skill. Clip: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BzsLbHoNXTs

*technical suggestion:* In class, play one short HealthyGamer clip only if you immediately pair it with the Lacuna or APA caveats. Do not let "MIT proved AI destroys brains" stand alone.

## Other recent vault clippings (cognition track)

### Lacuna secondary synthesis (best classroom companion)

*verified fact:* Lev-Tech / Lacuna, *AI and Critical Thinking: The Research* (2026-10-08). https://learnwithlacuna.com/articles/ai-critical-thinking-research

Maps four pillars students will meet in headlines:

| Study | What it actually supports |
|---|---|
| Lee et al., CHI 2025 (Microsoft + CMU), 319 knowledge workers | Higher AI confidence ↔ less critical thinking effort (self-report); work shifts to verification |
| Kosmyna et al., MIT EEG | See above; order-of-use is the usable takeaway |
| Fan et al., BJET 2025, 117 students | ChatGPT raised essay scores; knowledge gain/transfer did not beat other supports ("metacognitive laziness") |
| Gerlich, *Societies* 2025, n≈666 | Strong negative correlation AI use ↔ critical thinking via offloading; **cross-sectional**, cannot prove causation |

Lacuna's practical stack matches CVC method: draft your view first; protect judgment; watch confidence decay; practice unaided on purpose; do not confuse better output with better judgment.

### APA Monitor overview (July 2026)

*verified fact:* Abrams, Z. (2026). *How AI is reshaping human skills and thinking.* *Monitor on Psychology*, 57(5). https://www.apa.org/monitor/2026/07-08/ai-job-skills-thinking

Adds:

- Offloading is normal (lists, GPS); the new issue is offloading **executive** work (judgment, critique, creativity).
- Melumad et al. (*PNAS Nexus* 2025): AI-summarised research → shorter, less trusted advice than search-based research.
- Gerlich *Data* 2025: **structured** prompts that force independent reflection beat unguided ChatGPT and no-AI on expert essay scores.
- Lu et al. (*J. Applied Psychology* 2025): ChatGPT helped creativity more for workers high in metacognition.
- Budzyń et al. (*Lancet Gastro.* 2025): after AI entered colonoscopy workflow, unaided polyp detection fell ~6 percentage points (skill decay when assistance is removed).

*nuance / caveat:* APA stresses unanswered questions about lasting brain change. The teachable line is **how** people use AI, not a blanket ban.

### Mental health (adjacent, not the same thesis)

*verified fact:* Haber, N. J., *Emerging realities of artificial intelligence and mental health*, PMC13576966. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13576966/

- Therapy and therapy-adjacent chatbot use is real; models often fail bare-minimum crisis criteria.
- Sycophancy can reduce willingness to repair conflicts (Cheng et al., *Science* 2026, cited there).
- "Technological folie à deux": mutual reinforcement of delusions in long chats.

*claim / interview:* Huberman Lab Clips with Dr. Alok Kanojia (2026-03-24) on AI-induced psychosis and risk factors (heavy use, customization, mental-health use, preferring AI over humans). https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sqYLH72eCg0 Content warning for class.

*technical suggestion:* Keep mental-health harm as a **warning module** (Session 05 danger input or ethics beat). Do not collapse it into the critical-thinking skill argument without labelling the difference.

## Older automation literature (why this is not "new brain science")

Lacuna (and APA) correctly re-anchor the debate:

- Bainbridge (1983), ironies of automation: automate the routine → human keeps the rare hard cases without practice.
- Parasuraman & Manzey (2010): automation complacency / bias.
- Sparrow, Liu & Wegner (2011), Google effect: remember *where* more than *what*.

*nuance / caveat:* Offloading is how writing, books, and calculators already work. The course question is which cognitive work is the actual artistic / research job.

## What follows for Critical Vibe Coding

| Practice | Why |
|---|---|
| Student forms a claim or sketch **before** agent help | Matches MIT crossover + Gerlich anti-anchoring advice |
| Critique / verify agent output as graded skill | Matches Lee shift from producing → supervising |
| Unaided intervals (short live tasks without model) | Bainbridge: keep residual skill alive |
| Structured prompts over "do the whole essay" | APA / Gerlich: deliberate use can raise quality of thought |
| Label *claim* vs *verified fact* in every research note | Course style; also the metacognitive habit Fan says AI skips |
