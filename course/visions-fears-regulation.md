## Beat 1 — What drives AI development (~3 min)

**Say this frame first:**

> The industry needed a next growth platform after PC → internet → smartphone. It already held the **data** and the **compute**. AI is that bet — plus a race so no firm can pause alone.

| Driver | What to say | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Growth treadmill** | Large tech’s expansion history rides successive general-purpose platforms. After mobile, the next scalable product story became generative AI / agents. | *claim* (course political-economy frame; also in lecture block 3) |
| **Data + compute ready** | Decades of behavioural/textual archives + GPU clusters made large models commercially thinkable. | *verified direction* |
| **Monetise the archive** | Sell access to models as productivity / creative tools; lock users into APIs and clouds. | *claim* |
| **Race incentives** | Day-to-day motive is often **get there first** (status, valuation, lead) more than the utopia slide. Coxon to Stewart: utopia as cover mixed with naked ambition. | *claim* (interview) |
| **Technical drivers** | Scaling laws, open tooling, chat productisation — real, parallel to the money story. | *verified direction* |

**Do not** retell BIS / bubble numbers here — one gesture: “CapEx and circular finance: Session 01 block 4 / research note.”

## Beat 2 — Promises and fears from the industry (~4 min)

### A. The utopia pitch (abundance / less work / checks)

**Is “nobody works, everyone lives on UBI” really a thing?**

**Yes as public rhetoric — no as settled policy.**

| Voice | Position (short) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Elon Musk** | AI/robots → “age of abundance”; prefers **universal high income** (government checks), not merely “basic.” | Public posts / interviews; e.g. coverage of UHI rhetoric 2025–26 |
| **Sam Altman** | Long pushed AI-era redistribution (*Moore’s Law for Everything*, 2021 — tax capital → citizen payouts). Later: **less** enamoured of plain UBI; wants broader “shared upside” (equity / access to compute), not only fixed cash. | https://moores.samaltman.com/ · later interviews (2026) |
| **Salim Ismail** (response to Stewart) | Jobs may not bounce back; decoupling **work from income** might be the point; “next economic system.” | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ionrjPXLIYg |

**Jon Stewart’s joke** (keep the spirit, not a courtroom quote): we do enough capitalism that we might eventually capitalism our way into socialism — i.e. private race → public payouts. Ismail treats that gag as accidentally sharp.

**Class line:** Utopia talk is real in Silicon Valley discourse. It is **not** a funded social contract. Stewart/Coxon: if the future is “agency traded for a cheque,” who voted for that?

### B. The dystopia / existential pitch (same industry)

| Story | Content | Use in class |
|---|---|---|
| **Lab safety / x-risk** | Models may be hard to control; misuse (cyber, bio); race under-funds safety. | Coxon interview: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lnLDjXy2kRA |
| **Credibility problem** | Promise Star Trek **and** cite non-trivial extinction odds while raising hundreds of billions. Stewart: pick a lane. Ismail agrees on hypocrisy. | Stewart monologue: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S18jxWZntUo |
| **Democratisation of chaos** | Powerful tools in unknown hands — not only “superintelligence wakes up.” | Coxon / Stewart |

**Coxon hinge (teach this):** Utopian slides vs **incentives** — first-mover wealth and status drive the daily race; utopia is mixed with ambition. Fear of the public/state interrupting the race can outweigh fear of the technology itself (*claim* — interview framing).

### C. Two fear stacks (do not collapse them)

| Stack | Who emphasises it | Typical objects of fear |
|---|---|---|
| **Frontier / existential** | Some lab researchers, safety orgs, media | Loss of control, catastrophic misuse, race dynamics |
| **Social / institutional** | Social science, labour, media art, Global South critique | Deskilling, inequality, platform lock-in, bias/feedback loops, surveillance, ghost labour, ecology |

Both can be true at once. This course spends more time on the **second** stack (Sessions 9–10); name the first so students hear the industry’s own double message.

### D. Jobs — 60–90 sec seed (full handout elsewhere)

**Do not** spend the Session 01 quota on Amodei % or Spiegel collage. Point here, teach detail in Session 9 or assign the handout: [[../research/ai-jobs-rising-tide-productivity]].

| Beat | One line | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | Automation looks like a **rising tide** (broad, gradual), not a **crashing wave** (sudden occupation wipe) | MIT FutureTech, arXiv:2604.01363 |
| **Who already** | No mass unemployment yet; **22–25** in AI-exposed jobs (e.g. software) are the canary — less hiring | Stanford *Canaries* (Brynjolfsson–Chandar–Chen) |
| **Paradox** | Juniors get the **biggest productivity lift** from tools — and face the **weakest entry hiring** | Demirer et al. Copilot RCTs; Canaries |
| **Exposed ≠ replaced** | IMF ~40% “exposed” is not 40% fired | IMF SDN 2024 |

**Student chart (programmers):** Demirer et al. **Figure 6** — junior / short-tenure gains ~**+21–40%** completed work vs senior ~**+7–16%** (noisy). PDF: https://www.mertdemirer.com/Papers/Demirer_AI_productivity.pdf  
**Related (often mislabeled Stanford):** Peng et al. Copilot lab task ~**56% faster**, less experience → more benefit — https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590  
**Actual Stanford productivity×experience:** Brynjolfsson–Li–Raymond call-centre study (~+15% avg; ~+30% novices) — https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161  

YouTube that walks wave/tide + charts (verify claims against the handout): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGVz615AQLA (~13:20).

## Beat 3 — “Amplifier / just a tool” — and the counter (~4 min)

### Ismail’s take (you like this — then complicate it)

> AI is an **amplifier**, not a moral actor. Fire heats the house or burns it down. Governance = constrain harm, expand access to benefit. Don’t let frontier firms self-regulate; regulate **outcomes** (fraud, weapons, bio-misuse, liability), not “mathematics.”

That is the classic **tool** argument with a sharper word.

### Why social science pushes back

| Counter | One line | Pointer |
|---|---|---|
| **Not neutral infrastructure** | Systems are built from someone’s data, capital, labour, and deployment goals — they arrive already tilted. | Crawford / Joler; Zajko on AI & inequality |
| **Amplifies existing hierarchy** | Training on unequal society + deployment in welfare, policing, hiring → **feedback loops**, not random “misuse.” | Zajko, *Sociology Compass* 2022 — https://doi.org/10.1111/soc4.12962 |
| **“Conservative” AI** | Default tendency is to **reproduce** the status quo unless designed to disrupt it. | Zajko / Birhane line of argument |
| **Who holds the amplifier** | A teenager with a tutor-bot ≠ a hyperscaler with the model weights, cloud, and lobbying. Access rhetoric hides concentration. | Session 9 platform dependency |
| **Agency vs cheque** | Stewart: stripping agency (“go paint, here’s money”) is not the same as freedom — even if UBI were real. | Coxon interview |

**Class line you can own:**

> Amplifier is useful language — *if* you always ask **who owns the gain knob**. “Depends how you use it” is true for a chisel; incomplete for a platform that reorganises labour markets and grids.

## Beat 4 — What regulation can (and cannot) do (~3 min)

### Can / should (realistic)

| Lever | Example | Course pointer |
|---|---|---|
| **High-risk uses & bans** | AI Act risk tiers, prohibited practices | [[eu-ai-act-and-datenschutz]] |
| **Personal data** | GDPR / DSGVO stays in parallel | same note |
| **Transparency** | Synthetic media / chatbot disclosure | AI Act vocab in note |
| **Liability & misuse** | Fraud, weapons, bio, cyber — Ismail and Stewart partly agree here | — |
| **Market / industrial policy** | Export controls, competition, procurement | Lecture block 6 “credibility marketing” |

### Cannot (honest limits)

- Globally “slow all AI” with one switch (weights diffuse; open models; China/US race) — Ismail’s strongest point against Stewart’s “just slow down.”
- Guarantee UBI / UHI — that is **fiscal politics**, not a by-product of model release notes.
- Fix meaning/agency crises by statute alone.

### Double face of “we need regulation”

*claim (course):* Calling for laws can also be **credibility marketing** (“we are so powerful we need rules”) while compliance costs favour incumbents. Still: take enforceable duties seriously when students process personal data or deploy public agents — [[eu-ai-act-and-datenschutz]].

**Close the talk:**

> Drivers = growth + data + race.  
> Industry sells **abundance and apocalypse**.  
> Social science asks who pays, who works, who decides.  
> Regulation can bind **uses and data**; it cannot deliver utopia or erase maths — and it should not be left to the racers alone.

## Student takeaway (one slide)

1. AI is being built because of **industrial growth logic + race**, not because society voted for Star Trek.  
2. **UBI/UHI talk is real rhetoric**, not a delivered social contract.  
3. **Amplifier ≠ neutral** — ask who owns the stack.  
4. **Jobs:** rising tide + early-career canary + junior productivity paradox — [[../research/ai-jobs-rising-tide-productivity]].  
5. **Regulation:** uses, data, liability — see EU/AT note; distrust self-regulation and hype-regulation alike.
