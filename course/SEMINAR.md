# Seminar

This is the central page of the course. It describes what the group does together.
The technical notes support this work. They do not replace it.

## How the sessions work

Eight sessions, each 10:00 to 12:00, with a break of 5 to 10 minutes.
Each session has the same parts: check-in, a student input (in five sessions), discussion, and technical practice.
Sessions 01, 04 and 08 are exceptions. See the [course rhythm](SESSIONS/overview/course-rhythm.md).

## Check-in

Every session begins with a check-in. We listen only. Nobody fixes anything during the round.

Two prompts:

1. How was your morning?
2. What did you do with AI since we last met, and how was that experience?

Each person has 30 to 60 seconds. A visible timer helps. Themes can go on the board after the round.

## Screen-free discussion

A large part of the seminar is talking without screens.
Laptops and phones stay closed during check-in, student inputs, and discussion.
We open them for technical practice, and only if you want to take part in it.

## Student inputs

In sessions 02, 03, 05, 06 and 07 one student group brings a question from the humanities, social sciences or political economy. The input takes about 15 minutes. Discussion follows.

| Session | Input |
|---|---|
| 02 | Flattening |
| 03 | Closed versus open weights as power |
| 05 | How dangerous is AI really? |
| 06 | The ecology of AI |
| 07 | Privacy: what happens with the data? |

Groups are about four people. They choose their topic at the end of Session 01.
Use course research and the [bibliography](REFERENCES/bibliography.md), not only video.
Students who do not want to use AI can do research and investigative work for their input.
Starting points for each topic are in [THEORY](THEORY/README.md).

## Group agreements

We write our group agreements together in Session 01 and keep them on this page.
Until then, these are starting points for that conversation:

- We listen before we answer.
- We say what we see and feel, and we do not speak for others.
- Doubt and refusal are valid positions.
- Some things said in the room stay in the room.

## Questions we are carrying

We collect open questions here during the semester. Anyone can add one.
First questions come from the topics of the student inputs and from [THEORY](THEORY/README.md).

## Ways to participate

You can take part with AI, with AI used critically, or without AI.
Without AI, you can do research, investigative work, input presentations, and documentation of what you see.
The required work is the same for everyone: the personal manifesto, the [minimum path](PROJECT-WORK/minimum-path.md), and one input.
The minimum path does not require an AI tool. It asks you to name your choices and to give reasons.

## How disagreement is handled

We expect disagreement. We say what we mean, we ask when we do not understand, and we separate the claim from the person.
If a conversation gets stuck, the instructor can stop it and ask each person for one sentence.

## When technical demonstrations happen

Technical demonstrations and workshops come after the break, in the practice block.
They show one possible path. They are not required for assessment.
See [PRACTICE](PRACTICE/README.md).

## Confidentiality and care

Do not put API keys or private data into notes you share.
Ask before you quote someone from the room outside the room.
If a topic is hard for you, you can pass, leave for a moment, or talk to the instructor afterwards.
