# Admission criteria — waiting list

Guideline for filling places in Critical Vibe Coding when there are more applicants than places. Agreed with colleagues at Interface Cultures (IC), October 2026.

## Priority

1. **IC students first.** Places go to students enrolled at Interface Cultures. Non-IC students are welcome to apply again in later rounds, but IC students have priority.
2. **Older semesters before younger.** Within IC, students in higher semesters get priority. In the first round this meant: second year in, first semester out.
3. **Coding experience helps, but is not required.** Prior coding classes are an advantage for the course. Older semesters are more likely to have taken one, which is part of why rule 2 makes sense. No one is excluded only because they cannot code.

## Non-IC students

- Some students from other departments are wanted in the group, for variety and exchange between practices.
- Anyone already registered on the UFG list keeps their place. Students are not removed after registration.
- **Open for the next round:** define how many places are kept for non-IC students (a fixed number or share), and when non-IC registrations are accepted, so IC priority holds without registrations arriving first deciding it.

## Reasoning

- Students further along in their studies have less time left to take the course. Younger students will have later chances.
- The course is planned to run again. Being on the waiting list now means “not yet”, not “no”: IC students who are not admitted can join in a later round.
- Ideally, a year group takes the course together as a class.

## Process

- Check semesters against the UFG records before sending decisions.
- When a student’s semester is unclear, ask them directly instead of guessing.
- Send decisions only once the whole list is settled. Do not confirm individual places early.
- If a place was already promised before the list was settled, the promise stands. The student may be asked to wait for their year group, but the decision is theirs.
