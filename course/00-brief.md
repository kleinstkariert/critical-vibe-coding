# Critical Vibe Coding — seminar brief

*requirement.* Text below is the course brief given for this workspace (2026-10-15).

## Frame

This course combines hands-on prototyping with critical inquiry into AI-assisted and prompt-driven development. Prompting serves as a point of entry into different tools and production methods used in creative coding and digital fabrication. Students will explore visual and node-based environments, GUI-driven tools, text-based coding frameworks, and 3D/CAD applications, investigating how each requires different approaches to prompting, translation, and debugging.

Rather than presenting “vibe coding” as a frictionless shortcut, the seminar critically examines how LLMs and coding agents redistribute authorship, agency, and technical expertise. Practical work includes deciding when AI is useful or unnecessary, selecting appropriate models and tools, and comparing proprietary cloud services with local or open-source infrastructures.

These technical choices provide a starting point for discussing platform dependency, education, creative labor, future employment, political applications, and ecological impact. The seminar offers a shared space in which students can articulate and collectively examine their experiences, uncertainties, expectations, and concerns regarding AI. Students are encouraged to connect this work to their own artistic practice and ongoing semester projects.

## Objective

Students will gain insight into contemporary production methods through their own artistic practice, project work, and collective discussion, including:

- investigating the opportunities and limitations of prompt-driven development
- developing criteria for deciding when AI-assisted approaches are useful, when alternative methods are preferable, and which models, tools, and infrastructures are appropriate for specific tasks
- gaining orientation among different creative tools and technical approaches in relation to their own practice
- articulating and collectively examining experiences, expectations, and concerns regarding AI’s effects on artistic agency, education, and future work

## Teaching and learning method

Active learning activities:

- Input presentations
- Project-based learning
- Flipped classroom
- Debate
- Collaborative experimentation
- Peer feedback and discussion

Each class starts with a short group meeting and check-in round.

Students will be asked to give input presentations on different topics and tools.
